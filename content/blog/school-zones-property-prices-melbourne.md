The data is clear — school zones create measurable price premiums in Melbourne. ## How School Zones Work in Victoria

The Victorian Government introduced designated neighbourhood boundaries for government schools in 2021. Every government school has a defined zone, and students living within that zone have priority enrolment. Students outside the zone can still apply, but there's no guarantee of a place.

Key things to understand:

**Zones can change.** The Department of Education reviews and adjusts school zones periodically. A property that's in zone today might not be next year if boundaries are redrawn. This has happened and it causes serious grief for families who bought specifically for the zone.

**Being "in zone" doesn't guarantee enrolment.** Technically, the school must offer a place to every student in its zone. But if a school becomes overcrowded, the department can adjust boundaries to manage numbers.

**Selective-entry schools don't have zones.** Schools like Melbourne High, Mac.Robertson Girls', Nossal High, and Suzanne Cory are selective-entry based on entrance exams, not location. However, they do have designated neighbourhood zones that affect non-selective enrolment at nearby schools.

**Zone boundaries follow streets, not suburbs.** You can have two houses on the same street in different zones. Never assume — always check the exact boundary.

## The Hidden Risks of Buying for School Zones

### Zone boundary changes

This is the biggest risk. In 2022-2023, several Melbourne school zones were redrawn, leaving some families who'd bought specifically for the zone suddenly outside it. There's no compensation when this happens.

If you're paying a significant premium for a school zone, consider how close the property is to the zone boundary. A house in the centre of a zone is safer than one on the edge.

### School performance changes

Today's top-performing school isn't guaranteed to stay that way. Principal changes, teacher turnover, funding shifts, and demographic changes all affect school performance over time. The school driving a $150,000 property premium today might be average in ten years.

### Overpaying based on zone alone

A school zone premium is only worth paying if you'll actually use it — meaning you have school-aged children or plan to. If you're buying as an investment, the premium reflects other buyers' willingness to pay, which can fluctuate. During periods of lower school enrolment (which happens cyclically), the premium shrinks.

### Lifestyle trade-offs

Buying for a school zone sometimes means compromising on other factors — smaller house, busier road, longer commute, older property. Make sure the school zone isn't the only thing driving your purchase decision.

## Beyond Government Schools

Don't forget that school zones only matter for government school enrolment. If you're considering private or Catholic schools, location is less critical (though proximity still matters for daily logistics).

Melbourne has a strong independent school sector, and many families choose private education regardless of which government school zone they're in. Factor in the total cost — 12 years of private school fees ($240,000-$480,000) versus a $100,000+ property premium for a government school zone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I enrol my child in a school outside our zone?**
You can apply, but the school only has to accept you if there are places available after all in-zone students are accommodated. Popular schools rarely have spare capacity.

**Do school zones affect apartment/unit prices too?**
Yes, but usually to a lesser degree. Families with school-aged children more commonly buy houses, so the zone premium is strongest in the detached house market.

**What if my property is in multiple school zones?**
Many properties are in zone for both a primary and secondary school. Some properties fall in zones for multiple primary schools — you can choose which one to enrol at.

**Do school zones apply to prep (first year)?**
Yes. Zone priority applies from prep through to year 12 for P-12 schools, or the relevant year levels for primary-only and secondary-only schools.
