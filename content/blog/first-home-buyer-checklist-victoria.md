Buying your first home in Victoria is exciting, terrifying, and confusing in roughly equal measure. There's a mountain of things to check, grants to apply for, and mistakes to avoid. This is the checklist we wish someone had given us — practical, no fluff, specifically for Victorian first home buyers in 2026.

## Financial Checklist

### 1. Know your budget — properly

Before you look at a single property, get clear on your numbers:

- **Pre-approval** — Get a formal pre-approval from your lender. This tells you exactly how much you can borrow and shows sellers/agents you're serious. Pre-approvals typically last 90 days.
- **Deposit** — Most lenders require 10-20% of the purchase price. A 20% deposit avoids Lenders Mortgage Insurance (LMI), which can cost $10,000-$30,000 on a typical Melbourne purchase.
- **Hidden costs** — Budget an additional 5-7% of the purchase price for conveyancing ($1,000-$2,500), building and pest inspection ($400-$800), stamp duty (if applicable), loan establishment fees, moving costs, and immediate repairs.

### 2. First Home Owner Grant (FHOG)

Victoria offers a **$10,000 grant** for first home buyers purchasing or building a new home valued at up to $750,000. Key requirements:

- Must be a new home (never previously occupied) or a substantially renovated home
- Property value must not exceed $750,000
- You must move into the property within 12 months and live there for at least 12 continuous months
- At least one buyer must be an Australian citizen or permanent resident
- You must not have previously owned residential property in Australia

**Important:** The FHOG does not apply to established (existing) homes. If you're buying a second-hand house, you don't qualify for this grant.

### 3. Stamp Duty Concessions

This is where Victorian first home buyers get the biggest benefit:

| Property Value | Stamp Duty Concession |
|---------------|----------------------|
| Up to $600,000 | Full exemption (you pay $0) |
| $600,001–$750,000 | Sliding scale reduction |
| Over $750,000 | No first home buyer concession |

On a $600,000 property, this saves you approximately **$31,000** in stamp duty. It's the single biggest financial benefit available to first home buyers.

### 4. Victorian Homebuyer Fund

The Victorian Government's shared equity scheme allows eligible buyers to purchase with as little as a 5% deposit. The government contributes up to 25% of the purchase price (or 35% for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander buyers) in exchange for a proportional share in the property.

Eligibility includes income caps and property price caps that vary by location. Check the State Revenue Office website for current limits.

### 5. First Home Super Saver Scheme (FHSSS)

You can withdraw up to $50,000 in voluntary super contributions (plus associated earnings) to put toward your first home deposit. This is a federal scheme that provides a tax benefit because contributions are taxed at 15% rather than your marginal tax rate.

## Property Search Checklist

### 6. Location research

Don't just research the house — research the location. For any property you're seriously considering, check:

- **[Flood risk](/blog/flood-zone-check-victoria)** — Is the property in a flood zone or Land Subject to Inundation overlay?
- **[Bushfire risk](/blog/bushfire-risk-check-victoria)** — Does it have a Bushfire Management Overlay or Wildfire Management Overlay?
- **[Crime statistics](/blog/crime-rate-by-suburb-victoria)** — What's the crime rate in the suburb relative to the Melbourne average?
- **[Planning overlays](/blog/planning-overlays-victoria-explained)** — Are there heritage overlays, environmental significance overlays, or development restrictions?
- **[School zones](/blog/school-zones-property-prices-melbourne)** — Even if you don't have kids yet, school zones affect resale value

### 7. Transport and commute

Your daily commute will define your lifestyle for years. Check:

- Distance to nearest train station (and which line)
- Bus routes and frequency
- Drive time to your workplace during peak hours (test it on a weekday morning)
- Is the area likely to benefit from future transport projects? (Suburban Rail Loop, Level Crossing Removals)

### 8. Future development

Check what's planned for the area:

- **Planning applications** — Search your local council's planning register for development applications nearby
- **Zoning** — Is the surrounding area zoned for residential or does it include commercial/industrial zones that could bring noise, traffic, or unwanted development?
- **Infrastructure projects** — Major road upgrades, new schools, shopping centre developments, and train station upgrades all affect property values

## Inspection Checklist

### 9. Building and pest inspection

**Never skip this.** A $400-$800 building inspection can save you tens of thousands in unexpected repair costs.

The inspector should check:
- Structural integrity (foundations, walls, roof framing)
- Moisture and rising damp
- Roof condition (tiles, flashing, gutters)
- Plumbing (visible pipes, water pressure, drainage)
- Electrical (switchboard age, safety switches, wiring condition)
- Pest activity (termites, borers, rodents)
- Asbestos presence (common in pre-1990 homes)
- Subfloor condition (stumps, bearers, ventilation)

### 10. Conveyancer/solicitor review

Your conveyancer will review the Section 32 (vendor's statement) which includes:

- Title details and any encumbrances (easements, covenants, caveats)
- Planning information and zoning
- Building permits and certificates of occupancy
- Owners corporation details (for apartments/townhouses)
- Outstanding rates or charges

**Red flags in a Section 32:** Unregistered building works, easements that restrict what you can do with the property, owners corporation special levies, contamination notices, or Aboriginal cultural heritage sensitivity.

### 11. Strata/owners corporation check (apartments and townhouses)

If you're buying into a strata scheme, request the owners corporation records:

- Annual fees and what they cover
- Sinking fund balance (is there enough for future maintenance?)
- Minutes of recent meetings (are there ongoing disputes?)
- Planned special levies or major works
- Building defect history
- Insurance certificate

## Settlement Checklist

### 12. Before settlement day

- **Home insurance** — Arrange building insurance to start from settlement date. The property becomes your responsibility at settlement, not when you move in.
- **Utilities** — Arrange connection of electricity, gas, water, and internet. Book installations early as they can take weeks.
- **Council rates** — These will be adjusted at settlement. Budget for quarterly rates going forward.
- **Lock changes** — Change all locks on settlement day. You don't know how many copies of the existing keys are floating around.

### 13. Final inspection

You're entitled to a pre-settlement inspection, usually on the morning of settlement. Check:

- All fixtures and fittings included in the contract are present
- No new damage since your last visit
- Appliances work (test the oven, dishwasher, heating/cooling)
- All rubbish has been removed
- Gardens are in reasonable condition

## Common First Home Buyer Mistakes

**Buying at the top of your budget** — Just because the bank will lend you $800,000 doesn't mean you should borrow $800,000. Leave a buffer for rate rises, unexpected repairs, and life changes.

**Skipping the building inspection** — Especially common at auctions where the pressure to secure the property overrides caution. Budget for a pre-auction inspection.

**Ignoring body corporate issues** — That cheap apartment might have a $20,000 special levy coming for cladding rectification. Always check the owners corporation records.

**Forgetting about renovation costs** — A "renovator's delight" that needs $80,000 of work isn't a bargain if it would cost the same as a move-in ready home. Get [renovation quotes checked](https://tradietruth.com.au) before you commit.

**Emotional bidding** — Setting a maximum price and sticking to it is easy in theory and incredibly hard when the auctioneer is pointing at you and someone just outbid you by $1,000. Decide your limit before auction day and physically walk away if it's exceeded.

**Not researching the location** — You can renovate a kitchen. You can't move a house away from a flood zone or a busy highway. Location factors are permanent.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use the First Home Owner Grant for an established home?**
No. The Victorian FHOG only applies to new homes (never previously occupied) or substantially renovated homes valued at up to $750,000.

**Do I pay stamp duty on a $550,000 first home?**
No. First home buyers in Victoria pay zero stamp duty on properties valued at $600,000 or less.

**Can I buy a first home with a 5% deposit?**
Yes, through the Victorian Homebuyer Fund (shared equity) or through lenders that offer 5% deposit loans (you'll pay LMI). The federal Home Guarantee Scheme also allows 5% deposits without LMI for eligible buyers.

**How long does settlement take?**
Standard settlement in Victoria is 30-60 days from contract signing. 30 days is most common. You can negotiate longer or shorter settlement periods.
