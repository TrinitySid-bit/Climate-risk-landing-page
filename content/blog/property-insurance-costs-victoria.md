Most buyers calculate mortgage repayments, stamp duty, and conveyancing costs before purchasing a property. Very few calculate the insurance premium — and that's a mistake that can cost thousands per year. In Victoria, the difference in home insurance between a low-risk and high-risk property can exceed $5,000 annually. Over a 30-year mortgage, that's $150,000. Here's what drives insurance costs and how to check before you commit.

## The Location Factors That Drive Insurance Premiums

Insurance premiums are calculated using a combination of property characteristics and location-based risk. The location factors that have the biggest impact in Victoria are:

| Risk Factor | Annual Premium Impact | Where It Applies |
|------------|----------------------|-----------------|
| Flood zone (LSIO/FO) | +$1,000 to $5,000+ | Properties in mapped flood overlays |
| Bushfire zone (BMO) | +$500 to $3,000+ | Properties in Bushfire Management Overlay |
| Storm/coastal exposure | +$200 to $1,000 | Coastal and elevated properties |
| Crime rate | +$100 to $500 | Suburbs with high burglary/theft rates |
| Claims history (postcode) | +$200 to $2,000 | Areas with frequent historical claims |

These are cumulative. A property in a flood zone with bushfire exposure in a high-crime area could face $5,000-$10,000+ in annual premiums for a standard home — compared to $1,500-$2,500 for a comparable property in a low-risk location.

## Flood Zones: The Biggest Premium Driver

Flood risk is the single largest location-based driver of insurance costs in Victoria. After the October 2022 Victorian floods, many insurers dramatically increased premiums for flood-affected postcodes. Some key impacts:

**Properties in LSIO (Land Subject to Inundation Overlay)** typically face premiums $1,000-$3,000 higher than equivalent properties outside flood zones. The premium reflects the 1-in-100-year flood risk that the overlay identifies.

**Properties in FO (Floodway Overlay)** face the steepest premiums — $3,000-$5,000+ above baseline. Some insurers won't cover FO properties at all, or impose $10,000-$20,000 flood excesses that make the cover largely symbolic.

**Properties in SBO (Special Building Overlay)** face moderate increases of $500-$1,500, reflecting the overland flow flooding risk from stormwater.

The catch is that flood zone mapping isn't always current. Some studies are 20+ years old, and climate change is shifting rainfall patterns. A property just outside the official flood overlay today might be within it when maps are updated. Check the age of your area's flood study — NestCheck premium reports include this information.

## Bushfire Zones: Escalating Costs

Properties within the Bushfire Management Overlay face elevated premiums that scale with the Bushfire Attack Level:

- **BAL-LOW to BAL-12.5:** Modest increase, often $200-$500 above baseline
- **BAL-19 to BAL-29:** Significant increase, $500-$2,000 above baseline
- **BAL-40:** Substantial increase, $2,000-$3,000+
- **BAL-FZ (Flame Zone):** Some insurers refuse cover entirely. Those that do may charge $3,000-$5,000+ above baseline with high excesses

Since the 2019-2020 bushfire season, insurers have been recalibrating bushfire risk pricing nationally. Premiums in high-risk areas are rising faster than general inflation, and this trend is expected to continue.

## Crime Rates: A Smaller But Real Factor

Suburb crime statistics affect home insurance through two mechanisms: direct risk pricing (higher burglary rates mean higher premiums) and postcode-level claims history (if lots of claims have been made in your postcode, everyone's premium rises).

The impact is smaller than flood or bushfire — typically $100-$500 per year difference between a high-crime and low-crime suburb. But it compounds with other factors and affects car insurance too if your vehicle is garaged at the property.

## Coastal and Storm Exposure

Properties on the coast or at elevation face higher premiums due to wind and storm damage risk. This is particularly relevant for:

- **Mornington Peninsula and Great Ocean Road** — Coastal exposure adds premium
- **Elevated suburbs** — Properties on ridgelines or hilltops face higher wind loading
- **Properties near mature trees** — Storm damage from falling branches is a common claim

## Can You Reduce Your Premium?

If you already own in a high-risk area, there are steps that may reduce your premium:

**For flood risk:** Install a sump pump, raise electrical systems above flood level, use flood-resistant materials below flood level. Some insurers offer discounts for documented flood mitigation works.

**For bushfire risk:** Maintain defensible space, clear gutters regularly, install ember guards, upgrade to bushfire-rated building materials. A BAL assessment showing lower-than-expected risk may help with insurer negotiations.

**For crime risk:** Install monitored security (alarm systems, CCTV), secure doors and windows to Australian standards, join a neighbourhood watch program. Some insurers offer 5-15% discounts for monitored security systems.

**General:** Increase your excess to lower the premium (if you can afford the higher out-of-pocket cost in the event of a claim). Bundle building and contents insurance. Maintain a claims-free record.

## The Insurance Gap Problem

A growing number of Australian properties are effectively uninsurable — the premium is so high that owners can't afford it, or insurers won't offer cover at any price. The Australian Competition and Consumer Commission has flagged this as a national issue, particularly in flood-prone areas.

If you're buying a property and can't get a reasonable insurance quote, that's a major red flag. An uninsurable property is a financially dangerous asset — one event could cost you everything with no recovery path.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can the seller tell me what they pay for insurance?**
They can, but they're not required to. Ask through your conveyancer or directly. Their premium gives you a baseline, though your profile (age, claims history) may differ.

**Does strata insurance cover flood and bushfire?**
For apartments, the body corporate's building insurance should cover these risks. Check the policy details — some strata schemes have inadequate cover or high excesses. Your personal contents insurance is separate.

**Will insurance costs come down if climate risk decreases?**
Theoretically yes, but insurers are slow to reduce premiums. Improved flood mitigation infrastructure (like drainage upgrades) should eventually flow through to lower premiums, but the timeline is measured in years, not months.
