A house near a train station is a dream for commuters and a nightmare for light sleepers. Melbourne's train network is a major factor in property values — but the relationship isn't straightforward. Being close to a station can add value, while being too close can subtract it. Here's what you actually need to know.

## The Property Value Sweet Spot

Research consistently shows that proximity to train stations follows a curve — not a straight line — when it comes to property prices:

| Distance to Station | Typical Price Effect |
|---------------------|---------------------|
| Under 200m | -5% to +5% (noise offset) |
| 200m–500m | +5% to +12% (sweet spot) |
| 500m–800m | +3% to +8% |
| 800m–1.2km | +1% to +3% |
| Over 1.2km | Minimal impact |

The sweet spot is **200-500 metres** — close enough to walk to the station in 3-5 minutes but far enough to avoid significant noise and vibration. Properties directly adjacent to the tracks (under 100m) often sell for less than comparable homes further back.

Premium lines like the Sandringham line (running through affluent bayside suburbs) command higher proximity premiums than lines running through less desirable corridors.

## The Genuine Benefits

### Commute convenience

This is the obvious one. Living within walking distance of a train station eliminates the daily car commute, parking costs, and traffic stress. For a household where both adults commute to the CBD, proximity to a station can save $5,000-$10,000 per year in fuel, parking, tolls, and car maintenance.

### Future infrastructure uplift

Melbourne's rail network is expanding. The Suburban Rail Loop will create new stations in suburbs that currently have no train access. Properties near planned stations tend to see price increases well before the station actually opens. The Metro Tunnel project has already boosted values around new underground stations.

### Less car dependency

Households near train stations typically need fewer cars. Going from two cars to one saves $8,000-$12,000 per year in registration, insurance, fuel, and maintenance. That's a genuine financial benefit that compounds over time.

### Rental demand

Investment properties near train stations consistently achieve higher rental yields and lower vacancy rates. Tenants — particularly younger renters and students — prioritise public transport access over other amenities.

## The Real Downsides

### Noise

This is the elephant in the room. Train noise is significant and varies dramatically depending on distance, track type, and barriers.

- **Wheel squeal on curves** — The worst offender. Trains negotiating tight curves produce a high-pitched screech that carries hundreds of metres. Sections of the Hurstbridge and Lilydale lines through inner suburbs are notorious for this.
- **Horn blasts at level crossings** — Trains are required to sound horns approaching level crossings. If the property is near a crossing, expect horns from early morning to late evening.
- **Freight trains** — Some passenger lines share tracks with freight services that run overnight. V/Line services also operate on some metro corridors. These are louder and less frequent, which can be more disruptive to sleep than regular rhythmic passenger trains.
- **Track maintenance** — Night works for rail grinding, tamping, and maintenance happen several times a year and are extremely loud. These are scheduled but unavoidable.

### Vibration

Properties within 50-100 metres of tracks can experience ground-borne vibration, especially with older track infrastructure. This is felt more than heard — a subtle rumble through the floor as trains pass. It's worse in weatherboard and older brick houses than in newer concrete slab construction.

### Level crossing disruptions

Despite the Level Crossing Removal Project (which has removed dozens of crossings), many remain. Living near an active level crossing means regular road closures, traffic banking up, and boom gate noise. Check whether your crossing is scheduled for removal — this is actually a positive signal for future property values.

### Visual impact

Train corridors aren't pretty. Graffiti, maintenance equipment, overhead wiring, and lineside vegetation management mean the view from a track-adjacent property is industrial rather than leafy. Fencing along rail corridors can also feel imposing.

## What to Inspect Before Buying

### Visit at different times

This is critical and most buyers don't do it. Visit the property during:

- **Morning peak** (7:30-9:00am) — trains run every 3-5 minutes on most lines
- **Evening** (6:00-8:00pm) — frequency drops but noise still present
- **Late night** (10:00pm-midnight) — last services, V/Line, potential freight
- **Weekend** — reduced frequency but maintenance works common

If you only inspect on a quiet Sunday afternoon, you'll have no idea what weekday peak hour sounds like from the bedroom.

### Check what's between you and the tracks

Barriers make a huge difference to noise:

- **Other houses/buildings** — Each row of houses between you and the tracks reduces noise by 3-5 dB
- **Solid fences/walls** — Brick or concrete barriers help significantly
- **Vegetation** — Dense trees and shrubs provide minimal noise reduction (looks nice, doesn't do much)
- **Elevation** — Being uphill from the tracks is better than being below them

### Check double glazing

If the property has double-glazed windows, previous owners already dealt with the noise issue. If it doesn't, budget $10,000-$25,000 to retrofit double glazing — or accept the noise as-is.

### Research the line

Different lines have different characteristics:

- **Loop lines** (Craigieburn, Upfield, South Morang, etc.) — Regular metro services, some sharing with V/Line
- **Non-loop lines** (Frankston, Sandringham, etc.) — Metro only, generally quieter
- **Shared freight corridors** — Check if the line carries freight trains overnight

### Check Level Crossing Removal status

The Big Build website lists all completed and planned level crossing removals. A crossing scheduled for removal within 2-3 years is actually bullish for property values — you'll get the construction disruption short-term but a grade-separated crossing (rail bridge or trench) long-term, which eliminates boom gate noise and traffic delays permanently.

## The Emerging Factor: Airport Rail Link

The Melbourne Airport Rail Link (when completed) will connect the airport to the CBD via Sunshine. Properties near Sunshine station and along the corridor stand to benefit significantly from improved connectivity. This is a long-term play but worth considering if you're buying in the western suburbs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do trains run 24/7 in Melbourne?**
No. Metro trains typically run from about 5:00am to midnight, with some late-night services on weekends. However, freight trains on shared corridors may run at any hour, and maintenance works often happen between 1:00am and 5:00am.

**Will the noise bother me?**
Most people adapt within a few weeks. Regular rhythmic noise (like passing trains) is easier to adjust to than irregular noise (like barking dogs). But if you're a light sleeper, think carefully about anything under 200 metres from the tracks.

**Does train proximity affect insurance?**
Generally no. Train proximity doesn't directly affect home insurance premiums. Flood risk, bushfire risk, and crime rates have far more impact on insurance costs.

**Are properties near removed level crossings worth more?**
Early data suggests yes — properties near completed level crossing removals have seen value increases of 5-10% above suburb averages, particularly where the removal created new open space or improved streetscapes.
