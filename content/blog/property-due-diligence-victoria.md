## Planning and Zoning

### What zone is the property in?

Victoria has over 20 residential, commercial, and industrial zones, each with different rules about what you can build, how high, and how dense. The most common residential zones are:

- **General Residential Zone (GRZ)** — Standard residential. Allows subdivision and multi-dwelling development up to 11 metres (3 storeys).
- **Neighbourhood Residential Zone (NRZ)** — More restrictive. Limits development to preserve neighbourhood character. Typically 2-storey maximum, stricter garden and permeability requirements.
- **Residential Growth Zone (RGZ)** — Encourages higher-density development, typically near train stations and activity centres. Your neighbour could build a 4-5 storey apartment.

Why this matters: If you're buying a quiet family home next to a property zoned RGZ, a developer could build an apartment block next door. Always check neighbouring properties' zones, not just your own.

### What overlays apply?

Overlays add extra rules on top of the zone. The ones that affect you most:

- **[Heritage Overlay (HO)](/blog/heritage-overlay-victoria-guide)** — Restricts demolition, exterior changes, and fence styles
- **[Bushfire Management Overlay (BMO)](/blog/bushfire-risk-check-victoria)** — Adds $20,000-$80,000 to building costs, higher insurance
- **[Flood overlays (LSIO, FO, SBO)](/blog/flood-zone-check-victoria)** — Higher insurance, building restrictions, floor level requirements
- **Vegetation Protection Overlay (VPO)** — Restricts tree removal
- **Environmental Significance Overlay (ESO)** — Protects habitats, may restrict clearing

A single property can have multiple overlays stacked. See our [full guide to planning overlays](/blog/planning-overlays-victoria-explained).

## Climate and Natural Hazard Risk

### Flood risk

Over 100,000 Victorian properties sit in mapped flood zones. The financial impact is real — higher insurance premiums ($1,000-$5,000+ extra per year), building restrictions, and a 3-10% property value discount. Check our [flood zone guide](/blog/flood-zone-check-victoria) for details on LSIO, FO, and SBO overlays.

### Bushfire risk

Properties in the Bushfire Management Overlay face construction costs $20,000-$80,000 higher than equivalent properties outside, plus significantly higher insurance. Some areas rated BAL-FZ (Flame Zone) can't be insured at all. Full details in our [bushfire risk guide](/blog/bushfire-risk-check-victoria).

### Storm and wind exposure

Coastal properties and elevated sites face higher wind exposure, which affects roofing requirements, insurance, and long-term maintenance costs. NestCheck premium reports include storm risk scoring.

## Infrastructure and Development

### Check what's being built nearby

Before you buy, search your local council's planning register for recent and current development applications within 500 metres of the property. A 6-storey apartment building going up next door will change your outlook — literally.

Most councils have online planning registers you can search by address or suburb. Look for applications lodged in the last 12 months and any that are currently under assessment.

### Upcoming infrastructure projects

Infrastructure can dramatically change property values — both up and down. Check for:

- **Level Crossing Removal Project** — Removes boom gates and creates new public spaces. Positive for values, but construction takes 2-3 years and causes significant disruption.
- **Suburban Rail Loop** — New stations planned across Melbourne's middle suburbs. Properties near planned stations are already seeing premiums.
- **Road projects** — A new freeway on-ramp is great for access but terrible for noise if it's 100 metres from the property.
- **Precinct plans** — Many councils have adopted structure plans for activity centres that allow much higher density than current development suggests. Today's quiet shopping strip could be tomorrow's 8-storey mixed-use corridor.

### Contamination and environmental issues

Check the EPA Victoria Priority Sites Register for any contaminated sites near the property. Former petrol stations, dry cleaners, and industrial sites can have soil and groundwater contamination that affects neighbouring properties.

## The Section 32 (Vendor's Statement)

Your conveyancer will review this, but you should understand what's in it:

- **Title search** — Confirms ownership and reveals any mortgages, caveats, or encumbrances
- **Planning information** — Zones and overlays affecting the property
- **Building permits** — Shows approved building work (and potentially reveals unpermitted work by omission)
- **Owner's corporation** — For apartments/townhouses, reveals fees, special levies, and building issues
- **Services** — Connected utilities and easements

Red flags include unregistered building works, easements through the middle of the property, contamination notices, and pending special levies for owners corporations.

## The Physical Inspection

No amount of online research replaces physically visiting the property — multiple times, at different times of day:

- **Weekday morning** — Traffic, school drop-off chaos, train noise during peak
- **Saturday night** — Neighbourhood noise, nearby venues, street activity
- **Rainy day** — Drainage issues, damp in subfloor, water pooling in yard
- **Walk the streets** — Talk to neighbours. They'll tell you things the agent won't.
